## Engineering problem

A local compost-extract producer needed to reduce the repetitive manual work involved in processing compost. The team’s task was to develop a system that supported extraction while considering preparation time, drainage, handling, and the quality of the resulting liquid.

The project combined a mechanical feed system, water delivery, filtration, and liquid collection within a constrained prototype budget.

## My contribution

I coordinated team meetings and deliverables, completed most of the CAD work, and produced some of the engineering drawings. My design work included seven SolidWorks subassemblies, with fabrication and testing to investigate power transmission and extraction.

The prototype, testing, and manufacturing package were team efforts. The drawings shown here provide context for the shared design and should not all be read as individually authored work.

## Design and fabrication

The prototype uses a DC-motor-driven auger to move compost through a mesh-lined sleeve. A spray nozzle directs water onto the moving compost, and a collection casing guides the liquid extract toward an outlet.

The design combined purchased parts with custom components:

- Aluminum rings and housing panels supported the auger and motor.
- Polycarbonate panels formed the liquid collection casing.
- Printed parts included a custom motor mount, funnel, and standoffs.
- A PVC support positioned the spray nozzle above the auger.

Material and manufacturing choices reflected geometry, loading, available tools, and budget. The manufacturing package includes assembly drawings, a bill of materials, and process routers.

## Prototype testing and results

The team compared spray-nozzle positions through repeated trials. Extract quality was assessed **qualitatively by color**, using the scoring method in the prototype memo.

The tested prototype produced liquid extract and separated it from the bulk compost. The lowest tested nozzle position received the highest rounded average color score in the reported trials.

This was a visual assessment of the extract, not a direct measurement of microorganism concentration or agricultural performance.

## Limitations and next steps

Compost sometimes clogged the feed funnel, requiring manual intervention. Variable hose pressure and spray aiming also affected the consistency of the tests.

A revised funnel with smoother transitions was identified as a way to improve feed reliability. Further testing could use more consistent water delivery and a more direct method of evaluating extract quality.

The project illustrates how CAD, manufacturing decisions, and prototype observations feed into the next design iteration.
