## Engineering problem

Sphagnum moss ejects spores through a rapid pressure-driven process. Studying the airflow and physical release mechanism requires both a computational model and an experimental setup that makes the motion observable.

At Pomona College, I worked with Professor Dwight Whitaker and two fellow physics students on this research. The work brings together fluid dynamics, mechanical fabrication, and experimental observation.

## My contribution

- Simulated compressible airflow during spore ejection in ANSYS Fluent and analyzed vortex flow.
- Designed and fabricated aluminum capsule models using Fusion 360, machining, and Arduino actuation, with support from the shop technician.
- Designed a thermal PID-controlled smoke emitter to visualize vortex airflow from the aluminum test models.
- Used MATLAB computer vision to analyze high-speed dispersal footage. Related particle-tracking work examined poppy seed velocities and dispersal range.

The physical models developed through repeated iterations and daily collaboration. These contributions were part of the research team’s broader investigation.

## Engineering approach

The computational and physical models address complementary questions. ANSYS Fluent provides a way to investigate the transient airflow, while the fabricated apparatus allows the release mechanism and visualization methods to be explored experimentally.

The smoke emitter adds a controls problem to the fluid-dynamics experiment: regulating a thermal process to support visualization. CAD, fabrication, actuation, and analysis all contribute to the same experimental system.

## Outcomes and continuing work

The work produced physical test models, flow simulations, and experimental visualization hardware. Iterating between design, fabrication, and observation helped guide changes to the apparatus.

Research is ongoing. The material shown here documents the development process; it does not establish a final validated model of biological dispersal.

## Broader research context

I also helped collect Sphagnum samples at Shadow Lake Nature Preserve in Renton, Washington, working with an ecologist to identify sample locations using ArcGIS and contributing to plant-distribution analysis. This connected the laboratory work with the biological system being studied.
